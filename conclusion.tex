\section{Conclusions} \label{sec:conclusion}

This work developed and demonstrated a hybrid SN–diffusion neutronics method for improved control
rod modeling in molten salt reactors. The method applies the SN neutron transport method in
localized correction subregions surrounding control rods while retaining the neutron diffusion
method in the remainder of the domain. Transport corrections are introduced through drift
terms and are iteratively coupled to the diffusion solution using an adaptive buffer region to
preserve flux smoothness and improve iterative convergence.

In 1-D slab test problems, the hybrid method substantially improved control rod worth predictions
relative to the neutron diffusion method, reducing discrepancies to within approximately 1\% of
reference multigroup OpenMC estimates. Average and maximum channel power errors were also reduced
compared to diffusion. However, keff discrepancies on the order of several hundred pcm
remain, primarily due to neutron leakage treatment and multigroup energy discretization effects
rather than deficiencies in the localized transport correction itself. The method does not converge
to the full transport solution over the entire domain because corrections are restricted to
subregions near the control rods.

In 2-D MSRE-based configurations, the hybrid approach improved reactivity and power distribution
estimates compared to diffusion while incurring approximately 4.9$\times$ the computational cost of
diffusion-only calculations. This cost increase is substantially lower than that expected for
full-domain $S_N$ transport, which would increase the number of unknowns by roughly
$N\left(N+2\right)$.

Importantly, the method recovers diffusion-like behavior in regions where diffusion is valid and
confines transport corrections to areas where angular flux anisotropy and strong absorption
dominate. This localized treatment makes the approach suitable for multiphysics transient
simulations involving control rod motion, where accurate rod worth and localized power
distributions are of primary importance.

Remaining discrepancies associated with boundary leakage and core heterogeneity are better
addressed through complementary techniques such as albedo boundary conditions and simplified $P_N$
methods. Future work will focus on incorporating such treatments and extending to transient
\gls{MSR} scenarios and larger 3-D configurations to further assess validity and scalability.
