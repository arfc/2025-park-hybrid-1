\begin{abstract}
High-fidelity neutronics methods are computationally expensive for time-dependent reactor analyses
without extensive computational resources. However, these methods are necessary to accurately model
and simulate strong angular effects on neutron fluxes near control rods.
This work introduces a hybrid $S_N$-diffusion approach for improving control rod modeling in molten
salt reactors over the neutron diffusion method.
The hybrid method combines the strengths of the $S_N$ and neutron diffusion methods by generating
transport corrections using the $S_N$ method near control rods while keeping computational costs
low elsewhere. An adaptive boundary coupling algorithm that couples the $S_N$ and neutron diffusion
solvers allows the hybrid method to adapt to the
transport correction parameters and preserve smooth neutron flux gradients across the interface.
We implemented the hybrid method in Moltres, a MOOSE-based multiphysics reactor analysis
application, leveraging MOOSE's robust finite-element solver infrastructure
together with Moltres' existing reactor modeling capabilities to reduce development time.
In 1-D and 2-D $k$-eigenvalue simulations, the multigroup hybrid method improved control
rod worth and channel power estimates relative to the multigroup neutron diffusion method.
This work lays the groundwork for accurate and cost-efficient simulations of asymmetric
reactivity insertion transients in molten salt reactors.
\end{abstract}
