\begin{abstract}
High-fidelity neutronics methods are computationally expensive for time-dependent reactor analyses,
particularly when computational resources are limited. However, these methods are necessary to
accurately model and simulate strong angular effects on neutron fluxes near control rods.
This work introduces a hybrid $S_N$-diffusion approach for improving control rod modeling in molten
salt reactors over the neutron diffusion method. The hybrid method applies the $S_N$ transport
method in localized subregions near control rods to generate
transport corrections while using neutron diffusion elsewhere, thereby avoiding the memory and
computational expense of full-domain $S_N$ transport.
An adaptive boundary coupling algorithm that couples the $S_N$ and neutron diffusion
solvers allows the hybrid method to adapt to the
transport correction parameters and preserve smooth neutron flux gradients across the interface.
The objective is not to recover full-domain transport accuracy, but to improve control rod modeling
in multiphysics simulations while maintaining tractable computational cost.
We implemented the hybrid method in Moltres, a MOOSE-based multiphysics reactor analysis
application, leveraging MOOSE's robust finite-element solver infrastructure
together with Moltres' existing reactor modeling capabilities to reduce development time.
In 1-D and 2-D $k$-eigenvalue simulations, the multigroup hybrid method reduced control rod worth
errors relative to multigroup diffusion and improved channel power predictions.
This work lays the foundation for accurate and computationally efficient simulations of asymmetric
reactivity insertion transients in molten salt reactors.
\end{abstract}
